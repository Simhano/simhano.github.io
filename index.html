I work on computational mechanics. Most simulations predict how a known shape deforms. My research runs the problem in reverse: starting from a medical image of tissue already deformed by gravity, I use differentiable finite element models to recover its stress-free shape and its stiffness.

Can we ever see an object in its unloaded state on Earth?

Not really. Gravity acts on everything, all the time. The beam below was modeled perfectly straight and stress-free, yet it sags the moment it exists here. Switch gravity off to see the shape no camera on Earth can record.

Medical images have the same problem: every scan shows tissue already deformed by its own weight. My research works backwards from that image to the unloaded shape.

About

Research

Fully differentiable framework for inverse identification of geometry and material parameters with application to determining stress-free configuration of soft tissues

Hyunoh Bae, Tianyi Hu, Adrian Buganza Tepole, Hector Gomez · arXiv:2609.18109, 2026 · PDF

Problem
MRI and CT scans show soft tissue after gravity and contact have already deformed it. If a simulation treats that image as the stress-free starting shape, every prediction built on it is biased. Neither the true unloaded shape nor the tissue's stiffness can be measured directly.
Approach
We make the entire nonlinear finite element solve differentiable (in JAX-FEM), so exact gradients flow from the predicted shape back to both the unknown geometry and the material parameters. Both are optimized together, using two or more images of the same body under different loads.
Result
On benchmark problems and on a patient-specific breast model built from MRI, with and without a stiff tumor, the method recovers the unloaded shape and the region-wise material properties from gravity-loaded observations alone.

What we learned

  1. Diverse loading beats strong loading. Observations that mix deformation modes, such as tension with compression or stretching with bending, identify the solution faster than larger loads of a single kind.
  2. More observations help. Adding observed configurations improves convergence, even when they share the same loading direction.
  3. A bad initial guess costs time, not accuracy. Poor starting material values lengthen the early iterations, but with informative data the method converges to the same geometry and parameters.

Earlier work, University of St. Thomas

  • 2021–2024

    Statistical volume elements for composites

    with Dr. Katherine Acton

    Composites are random at the microscale, so a single representative cell is not enough. I characterized mesoscale properties with statistical volume elements, using a reduced finite element stiffness matrix to cut the cost, and smoothed SVE microstructures with a low-pass filter to reduce artificial stress concentrations at their boundaries.
